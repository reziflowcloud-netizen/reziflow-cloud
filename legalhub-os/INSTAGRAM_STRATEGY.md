Last updated: 2026-07-08

## Source Guardrails

Use only confirmed product areas unless clearly marked:

- Confirmed: leads, clients, cases, documents, tasks, reminders, calendar, dashboard, payments/debt tracking, users, employees, restricted access, settings, custom fields/sections, document templates, import/export, webhooks, and setup-dependent intake paths.
- Mark as `Needs clarification`: final CTA, demo assets, proof points, prices, trial terms, security/GDPR wording, legal review of templates, production readiness of Meta/Instagram/Facebook/Telegram/Google Sheets/Dropbox setup.
- Avoid absolute promises: "never miss", "guaranteed", "fully automated", "official native integration", "compliant", "no setup required".

## Main Role Of Instagram

Instagram should work as the top and middle of the funnel for LegalHub CRM.

Its job is not to explain every product feature. Its job is to make agency owners, operations managers, lawyers, and legalization consultants recognize their current operational chaos and see LegalHub CRM as the specialized system built for that workflow.

Primary role:

- Create awareness around the hidden cost of running a legalization agency through Excel, WhatsApp, folders, calendars, and memory.
- Educate the audience on what a legalization agency operating system should track.
- Build trust by showing how LegalHub CRM models the actual workflow: lead -> client -> case -> documents -> dates -> tasks -> payments -> responsibility.
- Move qualified viewers toward a demo, workflow audit, or direct conversation.

Secondary role:

- Build founder/product trust through Building in Public.
- Collect objections, use cases, feature questions, and wording from the market.
- Create a lightweight public product memory: what problems LegalHub CRM solves and why it exists.

## Account Positioning

The account should be positioned as:

> The practical operations account for legalization agencies in Poland that want to replace spreadsheet-and-chat chaos with one structured CRM workflow.

The content should feel:

- specific to legalization and migration agency work;
- operational, not inspirational;
- diagnostic, not generic SaaS marketing;
- founder-led and product-aware;
- useful even before the viewer books a demo.

The account should not feel like:

- a generic CRM vendor account;
- a legal advice account for end clients;
- a page of feature announcements only;
- a motivational business page;
- a collection of broad "productivity tips".

## What A Viewer Should Think After Viewing The Profile

After visiting the profile, a qualified person should think:

- "They understand the daily chaos of a legalization agency."
- "This is not a generic CRM with a different label."
- "They know the difference between a lead, a client, and an active case."
- "They understand documents, deadlines, payments, responsible people, and status updates."
- "This could help us move from Excel, WhatsApp, and scattered folders into one workflow."
- "I should book a demo or ask them to look at our current process."

The profile should make the buyer feel seen before it asks them to buy.

## Funnel: From Reels To Demo Request

### 1. Reel Hook: Make The Pain Obvious

Use Reels to lead with a sharp operational moment:

- "A lead without a next contact date is already slipping away."
- "Excel can store a case list. It cannot manage a legalization case."
- "If only one employee knows the case status, your agency has a process risk."

Goal: awareness and self-recognition.

### 2. Caption: Translate The Pain Into Workflow

The caption should briefly explain what breaks:

- leads from multiple sources;
- unclear responsibility;
- documents hidden in chats;
- dates tracked in memory;
- payments and debt visible too late;
- status updates spread across tools.

Then connect the fix to a CRM workflow, not to a feature dump.

### 3. Soft CTA: Ask For The Current Workflow

Use low-friction CTAs for cold viewers:

- "Comment 'workflow' and we will send the checklist."
- "DM us how your agency tracks cases today."
- "Tell us which part breaks first: leads, documents, deadlines, payments, or responsibility."

Needs clarification: whether comments/DMs are handled manually, by automation, or through another workflow.

### 4. Trust Content: Show Product Logic

Use carousels, founder posts, and short product demos to show:

- how a lead becomes a client and case;
- what belongs in a case record;
- how documents and comments connect to the case;
- how tasks and dates surface upcoming work;
- how dashboards show income, debt, active cases, new clients, and upcoming events;
- how statuses, services, fields, and templates can be configured.

Goal: trust and product education.

### 5. Lead Generation: Offer Demo Or Workflow Audit

For warm viewers, use CTAs:

- "Book a free demo."
- "Request a workflow audit."
- "Send your current Excel/WhatsApp process and we will show where CRM structure can help."

Needs clarification: final primary CTA and sales motion.

### 6. Demo Framing

The demo should not be framed as "see all features."

Frame it as:

> "We will walk through how your agency can manage incoming requests, clients, cases, documents, deadlines, payments, and responsible people in one structured workflow."

## Themes To Use

Use these themes repeatedly:

- Operational chaos in legalization agencies.
- Excel as a list, not an operating system.
- WhatsApp as a communication tool, not a case-management system.
- Lead follow-up and next-contact discipline.
- Lead-to-client-to-case workflow.
- Complex client data in legalization work.
- Case status, comments, history, and responsibility.
- Documents connected to the right case.
- Legal stay, filing, appearance, predicted decision, passport expiry, and other important dates where confirmed in the product map.
- Tasks, reminders, calendar, and dashboard upcoming events.
- Payments, unpaid balances, and debt visibility.
- Team control: users, employees, assignments, restricted access.
- Configurable agency workflow: statuses, services, lead sources, custom fields, custom sections, templates.
- Import/export and spreadsheet migration.
- Building in Public: product decisions, workflow research, demo-building, founder notes, and lessons from agency operations.

## Themes Not To Use

Do not build the account around:

- legal advice for foreigners or clients;
- immigration law explanations that could be interpreted as legal guidance;
- guarantees about legal outcomes;
- broad GDPR/security/compliance claims without approved wording;
- "fully automated" migration, billing, partner payouts, or integrations;
- official Meta/Instagram/Google/Telegram claims without production-readiness confirmation;
- AI, OCR, e-signature, government API integrations, client portal, automatic billing, or department/branch features unless marked `Needs clarification`;
- pricing promises until pricing, VAT, trial, and packaging are approved;
- generic entrepreneur motivation;
- generic CRM productivity tips that could apply to any industry.

## How To Show CRM Without Boring Ads

Show the product as the answer to a specific operational problem.

Use this pattern:

1. Start with a messy moment: "The client asks for an update, but the document is in someone's chat."
2. Name the risk: "Nobody can quickly see the case status, latest document, and responsible person."
3. Show one CRM screen or workflow: case record, documents, comments, task, date, payment, or dashboard.
4. End with a practical CTA: "Want to see this with your agency workflow? Book a demo."

Formats:

- Before/after: "Spreadsheet row vs case record."
- POV: "You are the owner and five cases need attention today."
- Screen-led micro-demo: "Where should a payment reminder live?"
- Myth correction: "CRM is not only for sales teams."
- Mini teardown: "What is missing from this Excel case tracker?"
- Founder build note: "Why we separated User roles from responsible Employee records."
- Checklist: "Every active case should have these seven things."

Product screens should support the story, not replace the story.

## Building In Public Strategy

Building in Public should show that LegalHub CRM is being shaped around the real operations of legalization agencies, not around generic SaaS assumptions.

Use it to share:

- why the product is focused on legalization agencies in Poland;
- how the lead-to-client-to-case workflow is modeled;
- how product decisions are made around documents, deadlines, payments, and responsibility;
- what questions the team is asking agencies;
- small improvements in demo data, onboarding, statuses, templates, and workflow education;
- founder observations about spreadsheet, WhatsApp, and folder chaos.

Do not share:

- private customer data;
- unapproved customer names, screenshots, or metrics;
- security details that should not be public;
- roadmap promises that are not approved;
- legal advice.

Good Building in Public post types:

- "What we learned from reviewing how agencies track cases."
- "Why LegalHub CRM has leads, clients, and cases as separate records."
- "A product decision: case status is not enough without responsible person and dates."
- "This week we are improving how agencies think about spreadsheet migration." Needs clarification if tied to actual roadmap.
- "A question for agency owners: what is harder to track, documents or payment debt?"

## Posting Mix

Recommended monthly mix:

- 35 percent Reels for awareness and pain recognition.
- 25 percent carousels for education and saves.
- 20 percent product demos for trust and demo intent.
- 10 percent founder/Building in Public posts.
- 10 percent stories for interaction, objections, demos, and lead generation.

## Success Metrics

Track:

- profile visits from Reels;
- saves on checklist/education carousels;
- replies to stories;
- comments using CTA keywords;
- demo or audit requests;
- DMs with current workflow descriptions;
- objections repeated by prospects;
- which pains generate the most qualified conversations.

Avoid optimizing only for:

- views without qualified profile visits;
- generic engagement from non-agency audiences;
- viral legal-advice content that attracts end clients instead of agency buyers.
