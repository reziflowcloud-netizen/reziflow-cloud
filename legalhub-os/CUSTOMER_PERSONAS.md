# Customer Personas - LegalHub CRM

Last updated: 2026-07-08

Status: marketing foundation v1 based on `PROJECT_BIBLE.md` and `PRODUCT_MAP.md`.

## Persona 1: Migration Agency Owner

### Who They Are

The founder or owner of a legalization/migration agency in Poland. They sell services, manage the team, handle difficult clients, watch cash flow, and often remain involved in operations even after hiring employees.

They may be running the agency from a mix of WhatsApp, spreadsheets, shared folders, calendars, and individual employee memory.

### Main Pains

- Leads come from multiple sources and are hard to follow up consistently.
- Cases are active, but it is unclear which ones are stuck, unpaid, or close to a deadline.
- The owner has to ask employees for status updates manually.
- Important client data lives in chats, spreadsheets, folders, and personal notes.
- Payment debt is visible too late.
- New employees are hard to onboard because the process is not centralized.

### Fears

- A profitable lead will be forgotten.
- A case deadline will be missed.
- A client will complain because nobody knows the latest status.
- The agency cannot grow without the owner personally controlling everything.
- Employees will leave with operational knowledge stored in their own files or chats.

### What They Want

- A clear view of active cases, upcoming work, unpaid balances, and team responsibility.
- A repeatable operating process that does not depend only on the owner.
- A CRM that understands legalization cases without months of customization.
- A cleaner way to scale the agency.

### Why They Need CRM

They need CRM not just to store contacts, but to control the agency workflow: requests, clients, cases, documents, dates, payments, tasks, and responsible people.

LegalHub CRM is relevant because it connects the core workflow from lead to case management and gives the owner dashboard-level visibility into operational and financial signals.

### Likely Objections

- "We already use Excel."
- "Our team will not use another system."
- "CRM implementation will take too long."
- "We are too small for CRM."
- "How is this different from a generic CRM?"

## Persona 2: Lawyer / Legalization Consultant

### Who They Are

A legal professional or legalization consultant who works directly with clients and cases. They care about accuracy, document completeness, dates, comments, case history, and reducing mistakes during client work.

They may work independently or inside an agency.

### Main Pains

- Client data is too detailed for a simple contact card.
- Documents are scattered across folders and chats.
- Each case has different dates, status changes, comments, and document updates.
- It is hard to reconstruct what happened in a case when information is distributed across many tools.
- Follow-ups and reminders depend on manual discipline.

### Fears

- Missing a date that affects the client.
- Using outdated or incomplete client information.
- Losing track of documents or comments.
- Spending too much time on administrative searching instead of case work.
- Being blamed for a process gap that was caused by poor systems.

### What They Want

- A structured client and case record.
- Case status history, comments, documents, payments, and tasks connected to the right case.
- Reminders for important dates.
- A process that reduces operational mistakes.

### Why They Need CRM

They need CRM because legalization work has many moving parts: personal data, immigration-specific fields, case dates, documents, comments, status changes, and payments.

LegalHub CRM is relevant because it is built around client and case records rather than generic sales deals.

### Likely Objections

- "I do not want a sales CRM."
- "Our cases are too specific for software."
- "Templates and fields may not match our process."
- "I need flexibility for unusual cases."
- "Is it safe to store client data here?" Needs clarification: approved security/privacy claims.

## Persona 3: Client Case Manager

### Who They Are

The person responsible for daily case coordination: contacting clients, checking documents, updating statuses, creating tasks, following up, and keeping the agency process moving.

They may not decide on software purchases, but they strongly influence adoption because they use the system every day.

### Main Pains

- Too many tasks are hidden in WhatsApp, notebooks, calendars, and spreadsheets.
- Clients ask for updates, but the latest status is not always easy to find.
- Documents and notes are difficult to connect to the right case.
- Deadlines and reminders are manually tracked.
- They have to switch between tools all day.

### Fears

- Forgetting a client follow-up.
- Missing an internal task or deadline.
- Being overloaded because priorities are unclear.
- Looking unprofessional when client information is hard to find.
- Being forced to use a CRM that adds data entry without helping daily work.

### What They Want

- A clear list of what needs attention today.
- Easy access to client and case context.
- Reminders connected to cases and clients.
- Less manual searching.
- Clear ownership and status on each case.

### Why They Need CRM

They need CRM because case management is daily operational work. A structured system can show what is due, who owns the case, what status it is in, what documents are connected, and what was already done.

LegalHub CRM is relevant because it includes tasks, calendar, reminders, case statuses, comments, documents, and client/case records in one workspace.

### Likely Objections

- "It will take too much time to enter everything."
- "I already have my own system."
- "The CRM will be used to monitor me."
- "What if the fields do not fit the case?"
- "Will I see only the cases I am responsible for?" Supported concept: restricted access exists, but customer-facing policy needs confirmation.

## Persona 4: Small Agency Owner

### Who They Are

An owner of a small legalization agency, often with one to five people, who is still close to every lead and case. They want to professionalize operations but may be price-sensitive and worried about complexity.

### Main Pains

- Excel worked at first but now creates mistakes.
- WhatsApp is fast but not reliable as an operating system.
- There is no single source of truth.
- New leads and old cases mix together.
- The owner cannot easily separate sales work from active case work.

### Fears

- Paying for software that the team will not use.
- Implementing something too advanced for the current size.
- Losing data during migration.
- Spending time configuring a tool instead of serving clients.
- Becoming dependent on spreadsheets forever and failing to scale.

### What They Want

- A practical CRM that starts with the agency workflow.
- Import/export so the move from spreadsheets feels less risky.
- Simple enough adoption for a small team.
- A path from today's chaos to a more structured operation.

### Why They Need CRM

They need CRM before the agency becomes too complex to clean up. Even with a small client base, the number of deadlines, documents, follow-ups, and payment details can grow quickly.

LegalHub CRM is relevant because it supports core objects the small agency already uses: leads, clients, cases, tasks, documents, payments, and reports.

### Likely Objections

- "We are not big enough yet."
- "Excel is free."
- "We do not have time for migration."
- "Can we start small?"
- "What happens if we outgrow the limits?" Needs clarification: final packaging and upgrade motion.

## Persona 5: Operations Manager

### Who They Are

The person responsible for making the agency run predictably. They care about workload, ownership, deadlines, process consistency, reporting, and making sure employees follow the same workflow.

They may be an admin user rather than the company owner.

### Main Pains

- No clean overview of who owns which lead, client, or case.
- Status definitions are inconsistent between employees.
- Some cases move forward while others stall without visibility.
- It is hard to report on debt, income, new cases, and active workload.
- Employees use different tools and naming conventions.

### Fears

- Operational mistakes become management problems.
- The agency scales headcount but not process quality.
- Reports are created manually and too late.
- Restricted employees see too much or too little.
- The owner loses trust in operations.

### What They Want

- Configurable statuses, services, sources, fields, and templates.
- A shared operating structure for the whole team.
- Dashboard and reporting visibility.
- Team responsibility and access control.
- Repeatable onboarding for new employees.

### Why They Need CRM

They need CRM because operations cannot scale on informal coordination. A shared CRM gives the team common data, common statuses, defined responsibility, and a place to see work before it becomes a crisis.

LegalHub CRM is relevant because it includes settings for statuses, services, lead sources, case options, users, employees, restricted access, tasks, calendar, and dashboard/report views.

### Likely Objections

- "Will this match our process?"
- "Can we configure fields and statuses?"
- "Will employees follow the system?"
- "Can management see enough without micromanaging?"
- "Which integrations are actually supported?" Needs clarification: production readiness of each integration.

- case managers;
- legalization consultants;
- sales/intake users;
