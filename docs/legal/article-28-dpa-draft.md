# LegalHub Article 28 Data Processing Agreement Draft

Approval draft dated 9 November 2026. This agreement is not executed by opening or approving a pull request. Complete the parties, processing instructions, provider authorisations, measures and retention schedules below before signing or starting the covered customer processing. The obligations in this draft are proposed contractual obligations, not statements that every procedure or safeguard is already implemented.

## 1 Parties and scope

The customer identified in Schedule 1 is the Controller. The proposed Processor is LegalHub CRM JDG Valentyn Verbelchuk, NIP 9512609364, at the business address confirmed in Schedule 1, privacy contact office@legalhubcrm.com. The parties must confirm their legal identities and authority to sign.

This DPA supplements the service agreement identified in Schedule 1 and covers personal data processed by LegalHub on the Controller's behalf through the specified LegalHub services. GDPR terms have their statutory meanings. The Controller determines the purposes and essential means; the Processor supplies the agreed service on the Controller's documented instructions.

Processing for which LegalHub independently determines purposes and essential means, such as its own service-account administration, is outside these processor instructions and requires a separate applicable notice and legal basis. This clause does not permit the Processor to repurpose customer records. The actual activities determine the roles.

LegalHub's own internal synthetic QA organization is not an external customer merely because it has a separate CRM tenant. This customer DPA does not itself need to be signed between departments of the same legal entity. Relevant contracts with external infrastructure processors remain separate.

## 2 Processing description and duration

Schedule 1 defines the subject, nature, purposes, data types, individuals concerned, frequency and duration. Processing is limited to that scope. Adding an organization, Page, form, field category, recipient or materially different purpose requires documented instructions and any necessary amendments before the change.

The term follows the covered services and continues for any authorised return, deletion or lawful restricted retention under section 10. A service subscription is not authority to retain all categories indefinitely.

## 3 Documented instructions and Controller responsibilities

The Processor shall process the data only as instructed in this DPA, its completed schedules and subsequent authorised written instructions, including instructions concerning transfers. It shall not use the data for its own advertising, profiling, sale or unrelated purposes.

If Union or Member State law requires processing outside those instructions, the Processor shall inform the Controller of the requirement before processing unless the law prohibits that notice on important public-interest grounds. It shall immediately notify the Controller if it considers an instruction contrary to applicable data-protection law and suspend the affected operation while the parties resolve the issue.

The Controller shall establish and document the appropriate legal basis, provide required information to individuals, lawfully collect the data, authorise access, issue lawful instructions, and assess that the Processor offers sufficient guarantees. Meta permission grants do not replace a GDPR legal basis. The Controller remains responsible for the content and configuration of its forms and for its decisions about leads and clients.

Only the Controller representatives listed in Schedule 1 may change processing instructions. The Processor shall verify authority before disclosing, exporting or deleting data in response to an instruction.

## 4 Confidentiality and access

The Processor shall restrict access to people who need it for the covered work. Before allowing access, it shall ensure that those people have a binding confidentiality commitment or an appropriate statutory duty. Access and confidentiality obligations shall continue for as long as required to protect the data, including after their service role ends.

The Processor shall maintain appropriate authorisation and access-removal procedures. It shall not give another customer access to the Controller's records. Administrative and support access must have a documented purpose and appropriate controls.

## 5 Security of processing

The Processor shall implement and maintain measures appropriate to the risk under Article 32, taking account of the processing, technology, implementation costs and potential impact on individuals. The assessment must address confidentiality, integrity, availability, resilience, restoration after an incident, and regular evaluation of effectiveness. Encryption and pseudonymisation shall be assessed and used where appropriate.

Schedule 2 shall identify the applicable measures and their verified status before covered processing starts. It must distinguish tested code, deployment configuration, operator procedures and provider commitments. It shall not assert unverified certifications, encryption at rest, key-management systems, fixed recovery times, continuous backups or automatic deletion.

Material changes affecting protection shall be documented and communicated to the Controller. The Processor shall not materially reduce the agreed level of protection without addressing the resulting risk and obtaining any necessary instruction or approval.

## 6 Assistance with individual rights

Taking account of the nature of the processing, the Processor shall assist the Controller with appropriate technical and organisational measures to respond to requests under Chapter III GDPR. It shall pass relevant requests to the Controller without undue delay and shall not independently reject or decide them unless legally required or instructed to do so.

Assistance includes identifying the relevant tenant and records, retrieving or correcting data, applying restrictions, and carrying out authorised erasure or export. It must consider linked notifications, technical references, files and copies; a Lead deletion control alone is not a complete service-level deletion procedure.

The parties shall cooperate promptly enough for the Controller to meet its statutory deadlines. No automatic export or erasure capability is implied where the service requires an operator. The request register shall record authority, scope, action, exceptions and completion evidence without collecting unnecessary identity documents or credentials.

## 7 Personal data breaches

The Processor shall notify the Controller without undue delay after becoming aware of a personal data breach affecting the covered data. The notice shall describe the nature of the breach, known categories and approximate numbers of affected individuals and records, a contact for follow-up, likely consequences and measures taken or proposed. Information not yet available may follow in stages without undue further delay.

The Processor shall take appropriate containment and remediation steps, preserve necessary evidence securely and assist the Controller with its notification and communication duties. A notification is not an admission of liability. This clause does not replace the Controller's own assessment or deadlines and does not invent an unverified 24-hour incident-response service level.

## 8 Assistance with security assessment and authorities

Taking account of the processing and information available to it, the Processor shall assist the Controller with Articles 32 to 36, including breach assessment, impact assessments and prior consultation where required. It shall maintain records required of it by applicable law and cooperate with competent supervisory authorities.

Where legally permitted, the Processor shall inform the Controller about a binding authority request concerning the covered data before disclosure, assess the request and disclose only what is legally required. It shall document any prohibition on notice as permitted by law.

## 9 Subprocessors

No new subprocessor may receive covered data without the Controller's prior written authorisation. The initial specifically authorised entities, services, locations and covered data must be listed in Schedule 3. A provider appearing in source code or a public provider DPA is not, by itself, that authorisation.

If the parties later adopt general written authorisation, they shall also agree an advance notification procedure that gives the Controller a genuine opportunity to object before an addition or replacement. Until that procedure is agreed, specific written authorisation remains required. The affected new processing shall not start while an objection remains unresolved.

The Processor shall bind each subprocessor to materially equivalent data-protection obligations relevant to its work, including sufficient Article 32 guarantees. It remains fully responsible to the Controller for performance of those obligations by its subprocessors. The parties shall distinguish a processor/subprocessor from an external platform acting under its own controller terms.

## 10 Return and deletion

At the end of the covered services, the Controller may choose return or deletion of the covered personal data. The Processor shall carry out that choice and delete remaining copies, except to the extent Union or Member State law requires storage. Return shall use an agreed practical format and secure delivery method.

Schedule 4 shall specify categories, periods or objective criteria, the trigger, responsible operator, actual procedure and evidence of completion. It must address active data, notifications, delivery receipts, duplicate-prevention records, diagnostic records, provider copies and backups separately. For each lawful exception the Processor shall document the data, reason, access restrictions and ending criterion and inform the Controller as legally permitted.

Backup retention must have a defined maximum or objective ending criterion and restricted use. If a backup is restored, the Processor shall reapply relevant deletion and restriction decisions before resuming ordinary processing. Retention of keyed or pseudonymous duplicate-prevention records must be necessary, justified and included in the instructions; such records are not automatically anonymous or exempt from erasure.

The Processor shall provide a record of the completed actions and any remaining lawful restricted retention. It shall not describe disconnection, a tombstoning job or a deletion of one Lead as proof that every copy has been erased. The code's age thresholds do not set a customer retention policy without the completed schedule.

## 11 International transfers

The Processor shall follow the Controller's documented instructions concerning transfers and shall satisfy applicable Chapter V requirements before a restricted transfer, including relevant access from another country. Schedule 3 shall identify the actual legal entities, locations, roles, transfer route, applicable mechanism and evidence. It shall record any required assessment and supplementary measures.

The parties may rely on an applicable adequacy decision or valid safeguards where the requirements are met. A provider's published SCCs are not a substitute for identifying the parties, applicable module and processing, and completing any necessary assessment. This draft does not execute SCCs by mentioning them and does not assert that every transfer has already been validated. An EU database region alone is not a guarantee of EEA-only processing.

## 12 Information and audit

The Processor shall make available information necessary to demonstrate compliance with Article 28 and shall allow and contribute to audits, including inspections, by the Controller or its authorised auditor. The parties shall coordinate scope, access and confidentiality to protect other customers and security while preserving the statutory right to an effective audit.

Existing independent reports may support an audit but do not automatically replace necessary additional evidence. The Processor shall identify material deficiencies and cooperate on appropriate correction. Commercial arrangements shall not prevent legally required cooperation or effective audit rights.

## 13 Suspension and termination

If compliance cannot be maintained, the Processor shall inform the Controller and stop or restrict the affected processing as necessary while seeking a lawful resolution. The Controller may suspend affected processing or terminate the covered service for an unresolved material breach, subject to applicable law and the service agreement. The return, deletion, confidentiality and cooperation obligations continue as relevant.

## 14 Precedence and approval

This DPA controls the parties' processor obligations where the service agreement or public privacy notice conflicts with it. Mandatory law and applicable transfer clauses retain their precedence. Nothing limits an individual's statutory rights or an authority's powers.

Changes require agreement by authorised representatives in a written or valid electronic form. This is an independently drafted Article 28 agreement; it is not presented as the European Commission's unmodified standard contractual clauses or as an already signed agreement.
